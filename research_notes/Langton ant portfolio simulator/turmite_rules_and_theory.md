# Rule Space and Theory of Langton's Ant & Generalized Turmites (foundation reference for the simulator)

Research date: 2026-10-17. Scope: exact update rules, rule encodings, Golly's Turmites family, importable notations, named behaviors with rule strings, proven-vs-open mathematics, and simulation variants. All URLs were fetched live during this session unless marked otherwise.

## KQ1 — Exact update rules for classic Langton's Ant (2-state "RL" ant)

### Takeaway
Langton's ant is a relative-turn, 2-color turmite: at each step it turns 90° (direction dictated by the color of the cell it stands on), flips that cell's color, and advances one cell in the new heading. From a blank grid it shows three phases — simple symmetry (~first few hundred steps), pseudo-random chaos (~to step ~10,000), then an unending 104-step-period "highway" with drift (±2, ±2) that begins at step 9,977.

### Cited Findings
- Canonical rule (Wikipedia wording): at a **white** square turn 90° **clockwise** (= right), at a **black** square turn 90° **counter-clockwise** (= left); in both cases **flip the color of the square** and **move forward one unit**; the ant may face any of the four cardinal directions. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- MathWorld words the same system with the opposite color convention (black → turn right, white → turn left) and charges the flip "when the ant leaves a square". The two descriptions are chiral mirrors of each other; from a uniform grid they yield mirrored trajectories, not different dynamics. Simulator presets must fix one convention and document it. — [MathWorld: Langton's Ant](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LangtonsAnt.html); cf. [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- Precise per-step algorithm (standard formulation, matches the GollyGang encoding of the ant as `{{{1,2,0},{0,8,0}}}` = "write 1 & turn right on color 0; write 0 & turn left on color 1"):
  1. Read color `c` (0=white, 1=black) of the cell under the ant.
  2. Rotate heading: color 0 → 90° right; color 1 → 90° left.
  3. Write the flipped color `(c+1) mod 2` to that cell.
  4. Move forward one cell in the new heading.
  The Esolang formulation ("invert the cell; step forward; then turn according to the cell now pointed at") is the same system with the turn phase shifted by one step — entering-cell color always determines the turn, the flip is always charged on the cell being left; both descriptions produce identical trajectories. — [GollyGang Rule Table Repository wiki: TwoDimensionalTuringMachines](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines); [Esolang: Langton's ant](https://esolangs.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- Three behavior phases from a blank grid: (1) simple symmetric patterns for the first few hundred steps, (2) pseudo-random "chaos" until ~10,000 steps, (3) recurrent **highway**: a pattern of 104 steps that repeats indefinitely, displacing the ant 2 cells diagonally per period (directions ±45°, i.e. drift (±2, ±2)). — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [MathWorld: Langton's Ant](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LangtonsAnt.html); [Lutfalla 2025 (LLLR note)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10482)
- The chaos-to-order transition point is measured at **9,977 steps** (the GollyGang non-halting-busy-beaver entry for Langton's Ant: "9,977 steps before making a highway"); MathWorld shows the highway forming by ~10,647 steps; other sources round to ~10,000–11,000. — [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines); [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LangtonsAnt.html); [Luxembourg EML student report, 2021](https://math.uni.lu/eml/assets/reports/2021/langton.pdf)
- The ant was invented by Chris Langton in 1986, published in *Physica D* 22:120–149 ("Studying artificial life with cellular automata"). — [Wikipedia, citing Langton 1986 (PDF)](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/26022/1/0000093.pdf)
- The ant can be re-expressed as an ordinary cellular automaton on a von Neumann neighborhood where the "ant" square carries one of 8 colors encoding (cell bit × heading) — this is how Golly ships `Langtons-Ant.rule` (a RuleLoader table, not a special algorithm). Useful if the simulator wants a grid-only unified representation. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [Golly Help: RuleLoader](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/Algorithms/RuleLoader.html); [Gajardo, Moreira & Goles 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0306022)
- The ant is reversible: each of the three elementary phases (turn, flip, move) is individually reversible, so the full system state (grid + ant position + heading) can be stepped backward — a nice free feature for a simulator (undo/scrub). — [Esolang: Langton's ant](https://esolangs.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)

### Inferences
- A single `step()` implementation of (read color → rotate by table → write → advance) with a per-(state,color) turn table covers Langton's ant, all generalized ants, and all relative turmites; the classic ant is the degenerate case M=1 state, N=2 colors.
- The chirality trap (white→right vs black→right) means preset galleries copied from different sources can be mirror images of each other; normalizing all presets to one convention (e.g., GollyGang: color 0 → turn right) avoids user confusion.

### Gaps
- Golly's exact in-app "Turmites rules" help page could not be fetched from any hosted mirror during this session (404 at golly.sourceforge.net/Help/Turmites.html, raw.githubusercontent goll/golly, and an archive.org Golly install). The authoritative text ships inside the Golly app under Help; verify its exact wording there before quoting it verbatim in UI copy.

## KQ2 — The generalized turmite model and Golly's Turmites rule family

### Takeaway
The general engine is: N cell colors × M ant states, a per-(state, color) action of the form (write color, turn by a multiple of the grid's base angle, next state, move/halt). Relative turmites encode turns relative to heading (isotropic under rotation); absolute turmites encode compass moves. Golly's own ecosystem uses two encodings: a compact L/R/U/N rule-name shorthand for "simple" (cyclic) turmites, and the GollyGang triple notation `{write, turn, nextState}` for fully general ones.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: a turmite is a Turing machine whose tape is an infinite 2-D grid and whose head additionally has an **orientation**; each timestep it (1) turns by some multiple of 90°, (2) changes the cell's color, (3) moves forward one cell — with actions indexed by the (current state, current color) pair. Turn alphabet on the square grid: **L** (90° left), **R** (90° right), **N** (no turn), **U** (180° U-turn). Worked 2-state 2-color table from Wikipedia: state 0: color 0 → write 1, R, state 0; color 1 → write 1, R, state 1; state 1: color 0 → write 0, N, state 0; color 1 → write 0, N, state 1. — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite)
- Relative vs absolute: **relative** turmites ("turning machines") encode moves relative to current heading and are rotation-isotropic (Langton's ant is one); **absolute** turmites encode moves in compass terms ("north") and are literally 2-D Turing machines. Turmites in general are exactly equivalent in power to 1-D Turing machines. — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); rigorous universality proof: [Gajardo, Hellouin de Menibus, Maldonado & Moreira, "Nontrivial Turmites Are Turing-Universal", 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05547)
- Naming history: Langton's ants (1986) declared "equivalent to Turing machines"; Allen H. Brady independently invented "TurNing machines" in 1988; Greg Turk investigated the same object and A. K. Dewdney coined "tur-mites" in his Scientific American *Computer Recreations* column, September 1989. — [Wikipedia: Turmite, citing Dewdney 1989, doi:10.1038/scientificamerican0989-180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); [Turmite Wikipedia history section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite)
- **GollyGang triple notation (authoritative, verified)**: a machine is a table of M rows × N columns of triples `{A, B, C}`, written in Western reading order; `A` = color to write `[0, n_colors)`, `B` = movement code, `C` = next state `[0, n_states)`. Square-grid relative codes: **1 = forward (no turn), 2 = right, 4 = U-turn, 8 = left, 0 = halt**; codes are **additive** so 10 = left *and* right (splits into two ants — this is how Ed Pegg's splitting/annihilating turmites are encoded). Example — Langton's ant itself: `{{{1,2,0},{0,8,0}}}`. Run these via Golly script `Scripts/Python/Rule-Generators/Turmite-gen.py` (bundled in [Turmites.zip](https://gollygang.github.io/ruletablerepository/downloads/Turmites.zip)). — [GollyGang wiki: TwoDimensionalTuringMachines](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Absolute-movement notation (same wiki)**: triple `{A,B,C}` with `B` one of `'N','S','E','W'` (square), `'A'..'F'` (hexagonal), or empty `''` to halt. Example 5-state 2-color 2-D busy beaver: `{{{1,'N',1},{1,'',0}},{{1,'S',2},{0,'W',3}},{{0,'E',4},{0,'E',3}},{{1,'W',0},{0,'W',1}},{{0,'N',0},{0,'N',1}}}`. — [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Other grids' relative turn codes (same wiki)**: triangular: 0 = halt, 1 = left, 2 = right, 4 = U-turn; hexagonal: 0 = halt, 1 = no turn, 2 = left, 4 = right, 8 = back-left, 16 = back-right, 32 = U-turn (again additive for splits). Corresponding Golly scripts: `TriTurmite-gen.py`, `HexTurmite-gen.py`, `AbsoluteHexTurmite-gen.py` (Golly ≥ 2.2). — [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Wikipedia Commons image IDs encode the same triples in decimal**: e.g. spiral-growing turmite image `Turmite-111180121010-12536` decodes to `{{{1,1,1},{1,8,0}},{{1,2,1},{0,1,0}}}` (state 0: color 0 → write 1/no-turn/→state 1, color 1 → write 1/left/→state 0; state 1: color 0 → write 1/right/→state 1, color 1 → write 0/no-turn/→state 0). So a 12-digit decimal string = (write, turn, next-state) triples concatenated, state-major. The same naming covers 2s2c gallery pieces `120121010011` (semi-chaotic), `121021110111` (chaos→highway), `121181121020`, `180121020081`, `181181121010` (Fibonacci spiral). — [Wikipedia: Turmite examples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); [Wikipedia: Langton's ant, multiple-states examples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- **Golly's generic .rule mechanism** (what generated turmite rules load through): `.rule` files with `@RULE/@TABLE/@TREE/@NAMES/@COLORS/@ICONS` sections; `@TABLE` takes `n_states:`, `neighborhood:` (vonNeumann/Moore/hexagonal/oneDimensional), `symmetries:`, variable lines, and transition lines with inputs ordered `C,N,E,S,W,C'`; unmatched inputs leave the cell unchanged; `RuleTableToTree.py` compiles tables to fast trees. — [Golly Help: File Formats](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/formats.html)
- **Golly's built-in Turmites rule-name shorthand**: rule names are strings over the turn alphabet (e.g. `RL` = Langton's ant, `LLRR`, `RLR`, `RRLLLRLLLRRR`, and L/U/N-containing names such as `RLU` for multi-color ants), with one character per (state, color) pair; in the GollyGang/Wikipedia "generalized ant" tradition the cell color and the ant state both advance by +1 (cyclically) per visit and the letter gives only the turn. NOTE: the exact ordering convention of the in-app Golly Turmites help page could not be fetched online this session — verify in Golly (Help → rule families) before hard-coding an importer. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant (naming scheme section)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines); Golly help index (no hosted Turmites page): [golly.sourceforge.net/Help/index.html](https://golly.sourceforge.net/Help/index.html)
- The generalized-ant model proper (N colors, 1 state, cell color always cycles +1, turn letter per color) is due to Greg Turk & Jim Propp, analyzed in Gale–Propp–Sutherland–Troubetzkoy, "Further Travels with My Ant" (1995). — [arXiv math/9501233](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9501233); [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)

### Inferences
- A clean engine API: `step(world)`, with rule = table `[(write, turnCode, nextState) for each (state,color)]` and grid topology (square/hex/triangular) selecting the turn-code alphabet; halt as turnCode 0; splits as additive codes feeding a multi-agent layer.
- Import surface worth supporting: (1) L/R (and L/R/U/N) strings for generalized ants; (2) GollyGang `{{{...}}}` triple strings (lossless for arbitrary turmites, and the de-facto archive format of the Rule Table Repository); (3) decimal triple concatenations for Wikipedia-Commons compatibility.

### Gaps
- The exact character set and pair ordering of Golly's built-in Turmites rule-name shorthand (and whether it supports explicit "no move"/backward moves or only cyclic +1 state/color advance) could not be verified from any online mirror this session. The term "LLMetrics" mentioned in the assignment brief was not found in any source.
- Golly has no hosted "Turmites.html" help page (404); its turmite documentation lives in-app and in the Turmite-gen.py script comments.

## KQ3 — Community rule-string notations an importer should accept

### Takeaway
Four encodings dominate: (1) L/R turn-strings for generalized ants (with a base-2 numbering from Gale–Propp), (2) GollyGang `{write,turn,next}` triple tables, (3) decimal triple-concatenation IDs (Wikipedia Commons convention), (4) Golly L/R/U/N rule names; plus hex-grid letter notation (N/R1/R2/U/L1/L2) for 6-fold grids.

### Cited Findings
- **Gale–Propp rule-string**: a word of N letters from {L, R}; the k-th letter is the turn taken on visiting a cell in state k; on leaving, the cell advances to state k+1 mod N. Langton's ant = `LR` in this convention (equivalent to Wikipedia's `RL` up to which color is "state 1"). By symmetry it suffices to consider strings starting with L. Base-2 encoding: L→1, R→0 gives each ant an integer ("ant 2" = LR, "ant 98" = LLRRRLR, 7 states); ant 9 = LRRL, ant 12 = LLRR, and the 6-state symmetric ants are 33, 39, 48, 51, 57, 60 — exactly those whose binary expansion is divisible by 3 (the even-run-length property). — [Gale, Propp, Sutherland & Troubetzkoy 1995, "Further Travels with My Ant"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/9501233)
- **Wikipedia/Turk naming**: "for each successive color, a letter L or R indicates the turn; Langton's ant has the name RL". Multi-color examples with behaviors are named directly by their strings (see KQ4). — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- **Hexagonal-grid ant notation**: up to six rotations, written **N** (no change), **R1** (60° cw), **R2** (120° cw), **U** (180°), **L2** (120° ccw), **L1** (60° ccw); example rules `L2NNL1L2L1`, `L1L2NUL2L1R2`, `R1R2NUR2R1L2`. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- **GollyGang triple strings** (defined in KQ2) are the interchange format of the whole Rule Table Repository — every busy-beaver record on that wiki is given as a `{{{...}}}` literal that can be pasted into Turmite-gen.py; adopting this format makes the repository directly importable. — [GollyGang wiki: TwoDimensionalTuringMachines](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Golly rule-table format** for arbitrary turmites as `.rule` files (n_states, neighborhood, symmetries, transition lines) — the same repository hosts `.table`/`.rule` files; Golly's GollyAccessPoint offers a downloadable [Turmites.zip](https://gollygang.github.io/ruletablerepository/downloads/Turmites.zip) and [Langtons-Ant-nColor.zip](https://gollygang.github.io/ruletablerepository/downloads/Langtons-Ant-nColor.zip) generator set. — [Golly Help: File Formats](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/formats.html); [GollyGang GollyAccessPoint.html](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/blob/gh-pages/GollyAccessPoint.html)
- **Turmite bestiary convention** (2026 gallery): genome = one letter per color, L/R; "letter k = turn on color k"; 57 canonical L/R rules of length 2–6 after removing mirror twins; the gallery also ran 180 random genomes of length 8/10/12. Example long-genome behavior: `LLRRLRLRRLLL` (12 "states") escapes to a one-direction laser after ~150,000 steps; `LLRL` (4) escapes while `LLLRRLLL` stays imprisoned in a 15-cell room. — [A Bestiary of Turmites](https://williamhackspeare.github.io/turmite-bestiary)
- Scott Sutherland's "Generalized Ants" page (Stony Brook) distributes Propp's `ant.c` simulator and ant pictures indexed by the integer code — the origin of the base-2 numbering in circulation. — [Sutherland: Generalized Ants](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~scott/ants/); [Gale–Propp et al. 1995](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/9501233)

### Inferences
- Conversions are trivial and total: a generalized-ant string of length N (colors) = 1 state × N colors with write = (color+1) mod N, next state = 0, turn ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} mapped from {N?, R, U?, L}. Any L/R string ↔ GollyGang triples ↔ decimal ID. A robust importer should accept all four and normalize to one internal table.
- Canonicalization rule to adopt (from the 1995 paper): normalize mirror twins by writing the word with a leading L; this halves the preset space and matches the community's 57-canonical-rules census.

### Gaps
- No single standard exists for multi-state (M>1) L/R/U/N compact names — different galleries ad-hoc extend the generalized-ant naming; the only lossless cross-tool format verified is the GollyGang triple table.

## KQ4 — Notable named behaviors worth shipping as presets (with rule strings)

### Takeaway
A preset gallery can be built entirely from verified, copy-pasteable rules: the classic RL ant and its generalized-ant relatives (Wikipedia's curated examples), the GollyGang busy-beaver/extinction records, the NKS absolute machines, and the new Lutfalla/Gajardo/Rao highway-family ants.

### Cited Findings

*Generalized ants (1 state, N colors, L/R strings; blank-grid starts):*
| Rule string | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `RL` (Gale–Propp: `LR`) | classic ant: ~500 steps of symmetry → chaos to ~9,977 → 104-step diagonal highway |
| `RLR` | chaotic growth; unknown if it ever builds a highway |
| `LLRR` (ant 12) | grows symmetrically (recurrent bilateral symmetry, ever-growing) |
| `LRRL` (ant 9) | same symmetric family |
| `LRRRRRLLR` | fills space in a square around itself |
| `LLRRRLRLRLLR` | convoluted highway |
| `RRLLLRLLLRRR` | filled triangle that grows and moves, after ~15,900 iterations |
| `LRRRRL`, `LRRLLL`, `LLRRRR`, `LLRRLL`, `LLLRRL`, `LLLLRR` | symmetric-growth family (even run-length property, binary codes divisible by 3) |
| `L2kR` family (e.g. `LLLR`, `LLLLLR`) | provably ≥ k−1 "harmonic highways" for the `L(2k)R` ant, k>1 |
| `LLRLRL` | provably **infinitely many** highways (periods 220+24n); no highway visible even after 10^10 steps from uniform start |
| `LLRRRL` | non-diagonal highways + "increasing rectangle" behaviours + infinite family of diagonal highways |
| `LLRLRLL` | infinite family of diagonal highways + "cone" behaviours |
| `L2NNL1L2L1`, `L1L2NUL2L1R2`, `R1R2NUR2R1L2` | hex-grid ants: circular growth, spiral growth, animation |

## KQ5 — Mathematical landscape: proven vs open

### Takeaway
Proven: the ant's trajectory is always unbounded (Bunimovich–Troubetzkoy 1992, commonly misattributed as the "Cohen–Kong theorem"); Langton's ant is universal/P-hard via circuit construction in its trajectory (Gajardo–Moreira–Goles 2002); all nontrivial turmites are Turing-universal (2017); several generalized ants provably build highways — including infinite families of highways for single rules and infinitely many highway-bearing rules (L2kR) (Gajardo–Lutfalla–Rao 2024). Open: the Highway Conjecture for the classic ant (every finite-support initial configuration ends in a highway).

### Cited Findings
- **Unboundedness theorem ("Fundamental Theorem of Myrmecology")**: an ant's track is always unbounded, for any initial configuration. Proven by **L. A. Bunimovich and S. E. Troubetzkoy**, "Recurrence properties of Lorentz Lattice Gas Cellular Automata", *J. Stat. Phys.* 67 (1992) 289–302 (doi:10.1007/BF01049035). The attribution circulated earlier ("Cohen–Kong theorem") is **incorrect** — stated explicitly by Gale–Propp–Sutherland–Troubetzkoy ("the attribution given there is incorrect; the proof actually first appeared in [Bunimovich–Troubetzkoy]") and echoed on Wikipedia. Gajardo–Lutfalla–Rao (2024) add: the proof is for rule LR but "it is easy to see that it is also true for the whole family of non-trivial ants." — [Gale–Propp et al. 1995](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/9501233); [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [Gajardo–Lutfalla–Rao 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10124)
- **Highway Conjecture (open)**: from any initial configuration with finite support, the classic RL ant eventually builds the periodic 104-step highway in some unobstructed direction. All finite configurations ever tested converge to it (it appears to be an attractor), but no proof exists. Corollary if true: any problem about the ant whose input is a finite-support configuration becomes decidable (iterate until the highway appears) — hence its importance. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [Gajardo, Moreira & Goles 2002, §1.2](http://www.dim.uchile.cl/~anmoreir/oficial/langton_dam.pdf)
- **Universality of the classic ant**: Gajardo, Moreira & Goles (Discrete Applied Mathematics 117:41–50, 2002; arXiv nlin/0306022, 2003) construct **any boolean circuit in the trajectory of a single ant** (NOT/AND/duplication/crossing gates on the 2-color lattice), proving: the system is **P-hard**, computes universally (it simulates any linear cellular automaton, hence any Turing machine — universality in the sense of simulating a UTM from an infinite, finitely-described initial configuration), and consequently has **undecidable** associated problems. Decidability for finite-support inputs remains open pending the conjecture. — [arXiv: Complexity of Langton's Ant](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0306022); [DAM PDF](http://www.dim.uchile.cl/~anmoreir/oficial/langton_dam.pdf)
- **Universality of turmites**: Gajardo, Hellouin de Menibus, Maldonado & Moreira, "Nontrivial Turmites Are Turing-Universal" (2017, arXiv:1702.05547) — the rigorous version of "turmites are exactly as powerful as 1-D Turing machines". — [arXiv:1702.05547](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05547); [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite)
- **Proven highway families (2024–2025)** — Gajardo, Lutfalla & Rao, "Ants on the highway" (arXiv Sep 2024; *Natural Computing*, May 2025, doi:10.1007/s11047-025-10018-9):
  - Theorem: the **L2kR** ants (rule words with k pairs "LL" then one "R"; k > 1) have at least **k − 1 distinct "harmonic" highways** — an infinite family of provably highway-building ants, indexed by k.
  - Theorem: the ant **LLRLRL has infinitely many highways** (e.g. a highway of period 220 + 24n for every n) — so the number of distinct highways of one rule need not be bounded. Simulated highway-appearance frequencies are very unequal (ratios as small as 1/10^7).
  - The key proof device: in these ants the heading parity over cell (i, j) is fixed by i+j mod 2 (widget constructions; reversibility forbids orbit-finite corners). — [arXiv:2409.10124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10124); [Springer/Natural Computing version](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11047-025-10018-9)
- **Limits of a generalized highway conjecture** — Lutfalla, "Sideways on the highways" (arXiv May 2025): the ants **LLRRRL** and **LLRLRLL** reach *both* highway and non-highway asymptotic behaviours from finite configurations (non-diagonal highways, increasing rectangles, "conic" behaviours, infinite diagonal-highway families) — so a generalized Highway Conjecture "generically does not hold" for generalized ants, sharpening how special the classic RL ant is. — [arXiv:2505.05426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05426)
- **Two known highways for LLLR** — Lutfalla (arXiv June 2025): formal definition of "highway" (finite configuration; n-periodic direction trace; trajectory periodic modulo an (a,b) drift); the LLLR ant has two distinct known highways; dynamics commutes with 90° rotations, so highway directions come in quadruples. — [arXiv:2506.10482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10482)
- **Symmetric-ants theorem (Truchet tiles)**: rule-strings whose cyclic runs of L's and R's all have **even length** (ants 9=LRRL, 12=LLRR, 33, 39, 48, 51, 57, 60 in base-2 numbering; equivalently binary IDs divisible by 3) exhibit **recurrent bilateral symmetry** forever — proved via Truchet tiles + the even-diagonal-degree property + Jordan curve theorem (Rümmler's switch picture; note: the argument genuinely uses planarity — it fails on the torus). Wikipedia paraphrases the sufficient condition as "the ant's name, seen cyclically, consists of consecutive pairs of identical letters LL or RR". — [Gale–Propp–Sutherland–Troubetzkoy 1995](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/9501233); [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant)
- **Busy-beaver landscape for turmites** (bracketed = steps / non-zero cells; square grid): relative 2s2c: 121/37 (Brady 1995); relative 3s2c: 21,790/2,793; relative 4s2c: 287,251/19,875; relative 2s3c: 59,348,218/888,629 (Fay 2018); absolute 2-D 5s2c: 25,772,988,638/935,508,401 (Gochev 2011); comparative 1-D values: 2s2c 6/4 (absolute), 13/4 (relative "flippers", Brady), 3s2c 21/6 absolute vs 82/9 relative, 4s2c 107/13 absolute vs 48,186/96 relative, 5s2c 47,176,870/4,098 (Marxen–Buntrock). Hex relative: 2s2c 54/24; 3s2c 57,867/1,654; 2s3c 59,823,304/227,345 (Fay 2018). Triangular relative: 2s2c 171/39 (Brady), 3s2c 4,499/1,611, 2s3c 21,500,468/44,594 (Fay 2018). Going absolute→relative improves scores (sole exception: 2s2c Sigma ties at 4); 1-D→2-D improves for most parameters; **no 3-D machine has yet beaten its 2-D counterpart**. — [GollyGang wiki: Busy Beavers](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **1-D universality context**: the 2-state 3-color 1-D machine `{{{1,'E',1},{2,'W',0},{1,'W',0}},{{2,'W',0},{2,'E',1},{0,'E',0}}}` is the Wolfram–Smith universal Turing machine (Alex Smith, 2007) — same table format as the repository's 2D entries. — [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Penrose-tile turmites**: on aperiodic tilings an infinite amount of information can live in the ant's *initial position* alone, allowing a "universal turmite" whose program is its starting location; consequently no busy-beaver analogue exists there (halting time is unbounded in position). — [GollyGang wiki: Penrose turmites](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Multi-ant interactions**: interaction modelling is a design choice whose outcome depends on the convention chosen (Belgacem & Fatès 2012 studied synchronous vs asynchronous multi-turmite collaboration robustness, *Complex Systems* 21(3):165–182); Ed Pegg Jr.'s variant lets one ant turn both left and right (splitting into two ants that annihilate on meeting), giving an alternative busy-beaver game. Ian Stewart's "The Ultimate in Anty-Particles" (Sci. Am. 271:104–107, 1994) popularized ant collisions as particle/antiparticle annihilation. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [Belgacem & Fatès 2012 PDF](https://hal.inria.fr/inria-00462438/file/AsynchRobustnessTurmites.pdf); [Stewart 1994 (archived PDF)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160303211426/http://dev.whydomath.org/Reading_Room_Material/ian_stewart/AntyParticles.pdf)
- **Empirical multi-ant parity folklore (not a theorem in fetched sources)**: a 2021 student study reports that same-cell ant stacks give #ants ≈ #highways (with a 10-ant exception), and that two ants displaced by an odd Δx/Δy fall into a repeating loop while even displacements build highways (or vice versa depending on initial headings). — [EML Luxembourg report 2021](https://math.uni.lu/eml/assets/reports/2021/langton.pdf)
- Related open problem: **Kotani's Ant Problem** (listed by MathWorld as a companion open problem) and Kotani-style questions about ants on *pre-colored* (e.g. random) lattices. — [MathWorld: Langton's Ant, see-also](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LangtonsAnt.html)
- Search tooling: GollyGang's [terminatingturmites](https://github.com/gollygang/terminatingturmites) repository is the program used to find current busy-beaver champions — reusable pattern for any "explore rule space" feature. — [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)

### Inferences
- Engine-count arithmetic (derivation, no source needed): a relative square turmite with M states, N colors, turn set {L, R, N, U} has (4·N·M)^(M·N) labeled rules — for M=N=2 that is 16^4 = 65,536 raw tables (before symmetry quotienting); generalized ants with N colors have 2^N words, i.e. 2^(N−1) after the leading-L mirror normalization. This bounds what an "explore all rules" mode can enumerate cheaply (2s2c fully enumerable; 2s3c = 24^6 ≈ 191M needs search strategies like terminatingturmites).
- Since universality holds for infinite configurations while the Highway Conjecture concerns finite ones, a simulator should be explicit that its "convergence" displays are empirical, not proven, for the classic ant.

### Gaps
- No fetched source proves the popular "even number of ants annihilate / odd number build a highway" statement as a theorem (only empirical reports and Stewart's popular account of collisions were found); treat it as folklore.
- "Behavior-count bounds for N-state turmites" as a published result (beyond the trivial enumeration arithmetic above) was not found in fetched sources.
- Exact publication venue/year of "Nontrivial Turmites Are Turing-Universal" beyond arXiv (journal version) was not verified.

## KQ6 — Variants worth simulating (and what each implies for the engine)

### Takeaway
One engine, one multi-agent layer, and pluggable grid topologies cover every established variant; the risky design choices are collision semantics for multiple ants, grid topology (torus vs plane), and absolute-vs-relative move encoding.

### Cited Findings
- **Multi-color generalized ants** (N colors, cyclic +1 flip, L/R word): the flagship generalization (Turk–Propp); trivial in the engine — it is the M=1 column of the general table. — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [Gale–Propp et al. 1995](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9501233)
- **Multi-state turmites (relative)**: add the ant's internal state as a second index; writes and next-states become free per cell. — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite)
- **Absolute turmites (2-D Turing machines)**: compass moves instead of turns; not rotation-invariant; needed for the NKS gallery and the big busy beavers (Gochev's 25.7-billion-step 5s2c machine). — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Hexagonal and triangular grids**: hex ants have a 6-letter turn alphabet (N/R1/R2/U/L1/L2); triangular-grid turmites go back to Brady (1995); both already have busy-beaver records in the repository — implies the turn-code alphabet must be a per-topology table (square 4 codes; triangle 4; hex 7 including splits). — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [GollyGang wiki: Triangular & Hexagonal Turmites](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **3-D (and higher) ants**: turmites have been considered in 3 dimensions with preliminary results collected (Tim Hutton); no 3-D machine outperforms its 2-D counterpart among known busy beavers; cubical lattices multiply storage cost. — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Multiple ants / splitting / annihilating** ("colony" mode): needs (a) a deterministic tie-break for simultaneous occupation (or simultaneous-update semantics), (b) annihilation on meeting (Pegg), (c) splitting via additive turn codes (10 = L+R spawns two ants); results strongly depend on these choices, and synchronous vs asynchronous update changes robustness (Belgacem–Fatès). — [Wikipedia: Langton's ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langton%27s_ant); [Belgacem & Fatès 2012](https://hal.inria.fr/inria-00462438/file/AsynchRobustnessTurmites.pdf); [GollyGang wiki: Extinction turmites](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Flippers (1-D relative turmites)**: Brady's one-dimensional relative machines on the integer lattice ("flippers"); 1-D absolute turmites are just ordinary Turing machines — a cheap extra topology (width-1 grid). — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Paterson's worms**: turmites on the *edges* of a triangular tiling; already shipped in Golly as `Worm-*` rules — a candidate related-beasts tab rather than a core engine change. — [Wikipedia: Turmite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmite); [Golly Help: RuleLoader](https://golly.sourceforge.io/Help/Algorithms/RuleLoader.html)
- **Penrose/aperiodic tiling turmites**: position-as-program universality; interesting but implies arbitrary-neighborhood support; no busy-beaver stat. — [GollyGang wiki: Penrose turmites](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Moore-neighborhood (45°) turmites**: proposed-but-unstudied on the wiki (viewed as intermediate between von Neumann 4-way and hexagonal 6-way) — an easy "novel mode" for the app, flagged as unexplored territory. — [GollyGang wiki: Moore Turmites](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Toroidal / bounded grids**: the theory (unboundedness theorem, harmonic-highway constructions) is for the infinite plane Z²; on a torus the finite state space plus reversibility forces eventual periodicity, and the 1995 symmetry proof explicitly fails on the torus — so the UI should warn that torus mode changes asymptotics; Golly implements bounded grids as a standard option. — [Gale–Propp et al. 1995](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/9501233); [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines); [Golly Help: Bounded Grids listed in Help index](https://golly.sourceforge.net/Help/index.html)
- **"N-ants" (allowed to go straight)**: a 2018 variant where the ant may move straight every N-th step breaks the Cohen–Kong-style unboundedness argument and produces oscillators for most N, highways for others (e.g. the 41-ant enters an 82-step highway only after 280,085,922 chaotic steps) — a one-line engine variant (add "S" to the turn alphabet with periodic gating). — ["Dynamics of Langton's ant allowed to periodically go straight", arXiv:1807.08789 (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08789)
- **Absolute + relative hybrids**: the same triple format already expresses both (letters vs digit codes); the UI can expose "absolute" as a mode flag rather than a separate engine. — [GollyGang wiki](https://github.com/GollyGang/ruletablerepository/wiki/TwoDimensionalTuringMachines)
- **Colony demos**: Langton's own demonstration of multiple ants interacting in a colony (video) is the canonical multi-ant showcase to replicate as a preset scene. — [Wikipedia external link: Chris Langton demonstrating multiple ants (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w6XQQhCgq5c)

### Inferences
- Architecture implication: keep the world model = (topology, cell lattice, list of agents with (state, position, heading)), one shared per-(state,color) action table for all agents (with per-agent override capability), and a semantics module for collisions (sequential order vs simultaneous vs annihilate). Everything in KQ4/KQ6 then becomes data + small flags.
- Backward stepping is free from reversibility (until an annihilating/splitting event is introduced, which breaks it) — worth gating the undo feature when extinction turmites are active.

### Gaps
- The term "flipped packets" from the assignment brief did not match any fetched source; nearest verified relatives are Brady's "flippers" and Pegg's splitting/annihilation packets (additive turn codes).
- Performance literature on optimizing 2-D TM simulation (beyond Golly's RuleTree compilation) was not surveyed in this session.
